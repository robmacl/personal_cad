LIGHT BOX v2 (for Robert's son) -- design notes
Started 2026-11-23. Units: inches as measured, mm in brackets.

PURPOSE
Phototherapy light box (seasonal affective). A NICOR mid/high-bay LED fixture stood on its short end,
with an extra diffuser panel in a frame in front of the lens so it can be looked at.
v1 (Robert's own): prismatic fluorescent-fixture diffuser in an aluminium-angle frame, RTV-bonded,
held to the light by four flat printed triangles (3D print/light_bracket.SLDPRT) pop-riveted to the
light. v1's weakness: the brackets are the only structure; top and bottom angles are just bonded trim.
v2 goals: sturdy enough to transport; corner reinforcement that covers the sharp extrusion ends;
spacers in the channel so the diffuser is located mechanically, not only by the face bond; feet.

THE LIGHT
NICOR HBL4155UV4K, 155 W, 120-277 V, 4000 K, CRI 80+, date 09/2023. Steel housing, powder coat,
frosted acrylic lens, vented driver channel on the back.
Datasheet (reference/NICOR_HBL4_spec_sheet_2026-03-16.pdf, page 3), HBL4105 and HBL4155 share a body:
  Length 23.8 (605)   Width 13.0 (330)   Height 3.6 (92)
That is the whole drawing: an envelope with L/W/H only. No side profile, no hole positions, no weight.
Anything finer has to be measured off the fixture.

THE NEW DIFFUSER (Robert's measurements)
Pre-cut sheet 24 x 13 nominal, about 0.030 undersize -> ~23.97 x 12.97 (608.8 x 329.4).
Thickness 0.114 measured (2.90), nominal 1/8.

THE CHANNEL (Robert's sketch)
Aluminium C-channel sold as 1/2 x 1/2 x 1/16.
  outside width 0.644 (16.36)   leg height 0.500 (12.70)
  slot width    0.507 (12.88)   floor 0.062 (1.57)   -> slot depth 0.438 (11.13)
  (0.644 - 0.507 leaves 0.0685 per leg, a little over 1/16.)
The diffuser is 2.90 thick in a 12.88 slot: 9.98 of slack, hence the spacers.

V1 BRACKET, measured from 3D print/light_bracket.STL
Flat triangle 61.1 x 63.5, 7.62 (0.300) thick, three countersunk holes: two about 38 apart on the
frame side, one about 48 away on the light side. Flat, so in v1 the frame's side face and the light's
side face are in one plane.

CONSEQUENCE FOR V2
Frame outside = diffuser + 2 x (edge gap + channel floor). With a 1.5 edge gap for RTV:
  about 614.9 x 335.6, against the light's 605 x 330.
So the v2 frame stands proud of the light by ~2.8 per side and ~5 per end. A flat bracket no longer
lies on both; the mounts need a step, and the frame (not the light) would touch the table first
unless feet set the base plane.

PRINTING
Bambu X1C (256 mm bed), Bambu PETG-CF. Channels cut on the chop saw to lengths from the design.

- wanted gap between lens and diffuser
- fastener to the light (rivets again, or screws so the frame comes off)

2026-11-23, second pass (Robert's replies)

DIFFUSER MATERIAL
Canal Plastics "Frosted Satin Ice" acrylic, custom size, 1/8 (0.118 / 3.0 mm nominal; 0.114 measured).

WHERE THE SHEET SITS
Against the inside of the channel's FRONT leg, as in v1 (bonded on the front face). The ~10 mm of
slot behind it is filled by printed spacers and by the corner tongues, so the sheet is trapped
mechanically front and back.

ADHESIVE (Claude's judgment; Robert proposed standard epoxy)
Acrylic grows ~70 ppm/K, aluminium 23. Over 609 mm that is 0.029 mm/K of mismatch.
If the sheet runs 20 K warm and the frame 10 K: ~1170 microstrain, ~3.5 MPa in the sheet if a rigid
bond holds it all round. Rough buckling stress for a 2.9 mm sheet 329 wide with held edges: ~2 MPa.
So a full-perimeter rigid epoxy bond is likely to make the sheet bow when warm. The bond itself would
survive (about 1 MPa average shear on a 10 mm land), so this is about flatness, not breakage.
Therefore: epoxy for printed corner tongues into the aluminium (that is the structure);
silicone (a thin smear, or dabs) between sheet and front leg, only to stop rattle and dust.
Not measured: actual diffuser and frame temperatures. v1 would tell: its sheet temperature after an
hour is the number to take.

EDGE ALLOWANCE
No glue gap. 1.0 mm per end on length, 0.75 per side on width (expansion ~0.45 / 0.25 worst case,
rest is cut tolerance). Frame outside becomes ~613.9 x 334.0 (was 614.9 x 335.6 with the RTV gap).

FASTENING TO THE LIGHT
Pop rivets, as v1. Robert likes them, and a 1/8 rivet drills out in a minute, which is repair enough.
Expected accident is a fall off a table: the frame stands proud of the light all round, so the
printed corners land first and act as bumpers.

CHANNEL SLOT
Nominal 0.500; caliper readings don't close (walls read 0.062 everywhere), slot may be off by up to
0.020. Slot width is the only dimension that matters. Settled by print, not by caliper:
  lb_fit_gauge (stl/lb_fit_gauge_2026-11-23.STL), 100 x 24 x 10 mm.
  Bar steps, handle end to tip: 13.1, 12.9, 12.7, 12.5, 12.3 wide -> try in the channel slot.
  Notches: marked (+y, small hole) edge 3.0 then 3.1; other edge 3.2 then 3.3 -> try on the sheet edge.
Print it flat in the same PETG-CF and settings as the real parts, so the answer includes the printer.
The tongues will carry crush ribs as well, so the fit survives variation along the extrusion.

WHY TWO PARTS PER CORNER
A one-piece corner with a leg hooked onto the bevel cannot go on: the bevels face outward-and-back
on both sides, so four hooks on a rigid frame would have to spread ~18 mm each to pass the side
flats. So each corner is a BLOCK (part of the frame) and a BRACKET (bolted to the light), joined by
two screws from the side. The frame comes off by undoing those eight screws, or the four M6.

CORNER BLOCK  lb_corner_block_A / _B   (A and B are mirror images; diagonal corners share a hand)
- L-shaped, 10.9 x 9.75 section, with a 30 mm tongue into each channel slot. Arms: 45 mm down the
  side, 20 mm along the end. The channels are square-cut and butt against the arms.
- The diffuser's corner rests on the block and tongues (z 25.25) inside a rim that stands 1.5 proud
  of the channel faces all round, with a 3 mm chamfer on the outside corner: this is the bumper.
- No printed lip over the FRONT of the diffuser at the corner. The channel front legs hold the sheet
  from 20 / 45 mm out. (A lip would need support material in a 3 mm slot.)
- Flat seat on the side face for the bracket, shouldered at both ends so the shoulders, not the
  screws, take load along the length. Two pilot holes, 2.7 dia x 10 deep, modelled undersize.
- Prints flat on its back (the face toward the lamp), rim up, no supports. 14.2 cm3.

BRACKET  lb_bracket_A / _B
- Constant profile, 26 wide: 7 mm leg up the side flat, 1.5 joggle out to the frame, 7 mm pad 36 long
  on the bevel. Leg crosses 15.5 mm of air between the lamp front and the block.
- M6: 7.0 x 13 slot (+/-3 along the slope), 17 dia x 1.5 relief for the insert flange.
  Use an M6 button head with a washer (12 mm or larger).
- Screw-head relief: 7 mm at the end-of-housing side, 4 deep, leaving 3 mm of leg over it.
- Two 3.9 clearance holes 16 apart for the screws into the block.
- Prints standing on its edge (the y = -33.7 face down, relief end up), so every layer carries the
  whole profile: the strong direction for the leg and the hook. No supports. 16.8 cm3.
- Leg check: 100 N sideways at the frame on one bracket, 20.4 mm arm -> 9.6 MPa, 0.13 mm.
  PETG-CF is good for roughly 40-50 MPa in-plane, so one corner alone takes ~400 N. E = 3000 MPa is
  an assumed figure.

- 8 x #6 (3.5 mm) thread-forming screws for plastic, ~16 long (7 through the bracket + 9 in the block).

2026-11-25, CHANNEL LENGTHS AS CUT, AND WHERE THE PROJECT STANDS (put to bed for a week or so)
Robert checked the four channels with a rule: three are remarkably close to nominal, one is about
1 mm short. His judgment: +/-1 mm is a fairly tight tolerance for a chop saw, and the design
should allow for more than that.
What that means here: the frame size comes from the LONGER channel of each pair; the short one
just shows a gap at one joint, which the 30 mm tongue and the epoxy cover. So a short channel is
harmless as long as the allowance round the sheet does not depend on it. With the old 1.0 / 0.75
allowance a 1 mm error was the whole allowance; with the 1.75 planned it is not.
When recutting: cut each PAIR clamped together so the two match each other, which matters more
than hitting the number.
